Project logic

Teaching students to shape systems

Students are encouraged to reason from problem framing to interface consequence.

  • Ask why the system exists.
  • Define context before designing.
  • Support decisions with evidence.
Critique

Feedback, iteration and clarity

Critique is used to help students make their work more legible, coherent and defensible.

  • Explain choices clearly.
  • Iterate through feedback.
  • Keep the human in view.

  • Reasoned design logic.
  • Awareness of use context.
  • Readable communication and structure.

What I try to teach

A teaching profile built around clarity, responsibility and usable systems.

Human-centered thinking
Understanding users, context and consequences.
  • Designing for human needs and operational realities.
  • Context before interface.
  • People before technical display.
Interaction quality
Reducing confusion and overload.
  • Readable information hierarchy.
  • Feedback and trust.
  • Low-friction interaction.
Applied design
From methods to actual systems.
  • Translate principles into choices.
  • Tie concepts to workflows.
  • Design with implementation awareness.
Research-informed teaching
Examples grounded in real work.
  • XR training systems.
  • Industrial interfaces.
  • Monitoring and digital-twin cases.
Critical reflection
Looking beyond novelty.
  • Why should the system exist?
  • Who benefits and who struggles?
  • How do we design responsibly?
Communication
Helping students explain decisions.
  • Presentations and critique.
  • Visual explanation of systems.
  • Reasoned design arguments.
