Teaching principles

What I try to teach

The course style follows the same values visible across the rest of the site.

Human-centered thinking
Understanding users, context and consequences.
  • Designing for human needs and operational realities.
  • Understanding context before building interfaces.
  • Connecting technical systems to actual use.
Interaction quality
Design that reduces confusion and overload.
  • Usability, readability and feedback design.
  • Clear information hierarchy.
  • Low-friction interaction in complex systems.
Applied design
From abstract methods to functioning systems.
  • Translating concepts into design decisions.
  • Thinking through workflows and tasks.
  • Designing with implementation in mind.
Research-informed teaching
Examples grounded in actual projects.
  • XR training systems and industrial interfaces.
  • Digital twins, monitoring and decision support.
  • Clinical and safety-critical scenarios.
Critical reflection
Looking beyond novelty.
  • Why a system should exist.
  • Who benefits and who struggles.
  • How to design responsibly.
Communication
Helping students articulate design choices.
  • Explaining decisions clearly.
  • Presenting design logic and evidence.
  • Connecting technical and human viewpoints.
